  1. AACL Findings
    Adaptation, Not Algorithm: LoRA and Full Fine-Tuning Show Comparable Black-Box Jailbreak Degradation in Five Open-Weight LLMs
    Taleef Tamsal and Jonathan Rusert
    In Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: AACL-IJCNLP 2026, 2026
    Accepted
    Abs Bib Code

    We fine-tune five open-weight instruction models (Gemma-2-9B-IT, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Phi-4, Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, and Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507) with LoRA, QLoRA, and full fine-tuning, and compare them with the unmodified base model and a 4-bit base. Across black-box, white-box, and multi-turn jailbreaks on HarmBench and JailbreakBench, scored by three automated evaluators checked against 750 doubly annotated human samples, benign fine-tuning raises black-box attack success by similar amounts whichever method is used. Under a ±5 pp equivalence bound fixed before training, LoRA and full fine-tuning are equivalent (TOST p = 0.014), QLoRA is more variable, and 4-bit quantization alone shows no meaningful degradation. Evaluator choice changes the conclusions: GPT-4o-mini’s false-positive rate on human-consensus safe outputs is 45.7%, against 8.6% for the HarmBench classifier.

  2. NeurIPS WS
    Your Judge Is a Confound: Evaluator and Attack Choice Distort Jailbreak-Safety Measurement for Fine-Tuned LLMs
    Taleef Tamsal and Jonathan Rusert
    In First Workshop on Reliable Evaluation for Language Models (JUDGe), NeurIPS 2026, 2026
    Non-archival. Also accepted at AdvML-Frontiers × CoTMA, COLM 2026.
    Oral Abs Code

    Accepted for oral presentation at JUDGe @ NeurIPS 2026.

    An empirical study showing how evaluator and attack choice can distort jailbreak-safety measurement for fine-tuned language models. Evaluator choice can change the conclusion as much as the adaptation method: even stronger GPT-5.6 judge configurations over-flag 21.5–29.9% of safe completions, and identical outputs receive different labels on 3–15% of repeat evaluations.

  3. SemEval
    PFW Task 8 at SemEval-2026 Task 8: Lightweight Tri-Fusion Retrieval with Prompt-Engineered Faithful Generation for Multi-Turn RAG
    Taleef Tamsal and Jonathan Rusert
    In Proceedings of the 20th International Workshop on Semantic Evaluation (SemEval-2026), Jul 2026
    Abs DOI Bib PDF Code

    A lightweight multi-turn RAG system that combines BM25, SPLADE-v3, and Jina Embeddings v4 with weighted reciprocal rank fusion, plus zero-shot GPT-4o/GPT-4o-mini prompting for faithful generation. The system ranked 6/26 on Task B, 14/29 on Task C, and 20/38 on Task A. Explicit citation-format instructions, not chain-of-thought alone, raised citation use from 4% to 93%.

  4. SemEval
    PFW at SemEval-2026 Task 6: Multi-Seed DeBERTa Ensembles for Political Response Clarity and Evasion Classification
    Taleef Tamsal and Jonathan Rusert
    In Proceedings of the 20th International Workshop on Semantic Evaluation (SemEval-2026), Jul 2026
    Abs DOI Bib PDF Code

    A multi-seed DeBERTa ensemble for political response clarity and evasion classification: 5-fold cross-validation with 10 random seeds yields 50 models per architecture, combined through logit averaging. The system achieves macro F1 of 0.76 on clarity (18/41) and 0.50 on evasion (12/33). Three post-hoc optimization techniques each improve out-of-fold performance yet degrade evaluation scores, suggesting a risk of overfitting to cross-validation predictions when evaluation data is limited.

  5. Thesis
    Do Efficient Adaptations Reduce Safety? Jailbreak Robustness of PEFT vs. Full Fine-Tuning on Consumer-Accessible LLMs
    Taleef Tamsal
    Purdue University Fort Wayne, May 2026
    Abs DOI

    A systematic study of 25 model configurations evaluated against four jailbreak attacks and two safety benchmarks, producing nearly 600 attack-success-rate measurements calibrated against a 750-sample human annotation study. The thesis examines how full fine-tuning, LoRA, QLoRA, and quantization change black-box safety and how evaluator choice affects conclusions.
